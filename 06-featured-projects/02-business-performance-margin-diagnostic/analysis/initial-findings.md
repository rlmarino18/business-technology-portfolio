# Initial Findings — Toast Business Performance Diagnostic

## Scope

This note captures preliminary findings from the 2023–2025 revenue, gross profit, gross margin, and segment-mix analysis.

These findings are directional and should not yet be treated as final management recommendations.

## Preliminary Findings

### 1. Consolidated gross margin improved materially

Toast's consolidated gross margin increased from approximately 21.6% in 2023 to 25.9% in 2025, an improvement of approximately 4.31 percentage points.

This indicates meaningful improvement in gross-level operating economics over the period.

### 2. Subscription economics strengthened

Subscription gross margin increased from approximately 66.8% to 71.8%, an improvement of approximately 4.99 percentage points.

Subscription also increased its share of total revenue by approximately 2.28 percentage points.

This suggests Subscription is becoming increasingly important to consolidated margin quality.

### 3. Fintech remains the largest absolute gross-profit growth engine

Fintech contributed approximately 60.6% of the total increase in gross profit between 2023 and 2025.

Its gross margin improved more modestly, by approximately 1.24 percentage points.

This indicates that Fintech remains the primary scale contributor even as its relative share of gross profit declines.

### 4. Hardware & Services remains economically dilutive

Hardware & Services generated negative gross profit throughout the period.

Its gross-profit contribution declined by approximately $39 million between 2023 and 2025 and represented approximately -5.1% of consolidated gross-profit growth over the period.

Its share of total revenue also declined by approximately 1.63 percentage points.

### 5. Margin improvement appears associated with both mix and segment economics

The current evidence is consistent with a combination of:

- stronger Subscription gross margins,
- modest Fintech margin improvement,
- increasing Subscription revenue mix,
- declining Hardware & Services revenue mix,
- and continued strong absolute Fintech gross-profit growth.

The current analysis supports association, not causal attribution.

## Current Management Hypothesis

Toast's gross-margin expansion appears to be driven by a combination of improving segment economics and favorable revenue mix, with Fintech providing scale, Subscription improving margin quality, and Hardware & Services acting as a persistent gross-profit drag.

## Next Analytical Questions

- What operating and commercial factors caused the observed mix shift toward Subscription and away from Hardware & Services?
- What operating-cost drivers sit below gross profit?
- Which margin-improvement opportunities are structurally repeatable versus temporary?
- What initiatives could improve Hardware & Services economics or reduce its drag?
- What implementation costs, risks, and payback periods would apply to each potential initiative?

## Gross Margin Decomposition

A decomposition of Toast's 2023–2025 consolidated gross-margin expansion shows that the improvement was driven primarily by revenue mix rather than by within-segment margin improvement alone.

### 2023–2025 Margin Change

- Mix effect: +3.06 percentage points
- Within-segment effect: +0.79 percentage points
- Interaction effect: +0.42 percentage points
- Amortization effect: +0.05 percentage points
- Total explained margin change: +4.31 percentage points
- Observed gross-margin change: +4.31 percentage points

Approximately 71% of the observed gross-margin expansion was attributable to favorable revenue mix.

The largest structural drivers were:

- increasing revenue exposure to the higher-margin Subscription business
- decreasing revenue exposure to negative-margin Hardware & Services
- additional support from improving Subscription and Fintech segment margins

This strengthens the current management hypothesis: Toast's margin expansion reflects both mix and operating-economics improvements, but the dominant observed driver from 2023 through 2025 was a more favorable business mix.

The decomposition is descriptive and based on reported segment economics. It identifies mathematical contribution to observed margin change but does not by itself establish the operational causes behind each segment's margin movement.
