# Executive Recommendation

## Management Question

How should CAVA allocate capital and organizational capacity over the next 12–24 months to maximize growth while preserving restaurant-level economics?

---

## Recommendation

CAVA should pursue a balanced growth strategy that prioritizes same-store productivity, maintains disciplined restaurant expansion, and selectively invests in infrastructure that improves the quality and scalability of future growth.

The analysis does not support maximizing restaurant-opening velocity as the primary objective.

CAVA’s restaurant base continues to expand rapidly, but same-restaurant sales growth has moderated and expansion appears increasingly capital intensive. At the same time, the existing restaurant base is now large enough that modest productivity improvements can create meaningful economic value without requiring equivalent new-unit investment.

Management should therefore allocate incremental resources according to the following priority:

1. Same-Store Productivity
2. Disciplined Restaurant Expansion
3. Scalable Infrastructure as an Enabling Investment

---

# 1. Prioritize Same-Store Productivity

## Rationale

Same-store productivity ranked first under Balanced, Growth-First, and Efficiency-First prioritization scenarios.

A modeled 3% productivity improvement across approximately 500 restaurants produces:

- Approximately $46.5M in incremental annual revenue
- Approximately $10.9M in incremental restaurant-level profit

This approaches the modeled profit contribution of accelerated expansion. However, the investment required to generate the productivity improvement is not observable from public data, so superior capital efficiency cannot be established without additional operating and implementation-cost information.

The exact investment required to achieve the productivity improvement is unknown and must be validated before implementation.

## Management Focus

Management should concentrate on:

- Restaurant throughput
- Labor productivity
- Customer frequency
- Loyalty engagement
- Digital workflow integration
- Menu and operational complexity
- Performance variation across locations

The objective should be to increase economic output from the existing footprint without relying primarily on pricing or compromising the customer experience.

---

# 2. Continue Disciplined Restaurant Expansion

## Rationale

Expansion remains strategically attractive and should not be materially reduced.

The modeled base case for 20 incremental restaurants produces approximately:

- $62.0M in mature annual revenue
- $14.5M in mature restaurant-level profit
- $49.3M in modeled initial investment
- 3.4 years of mature payback

Expansion therefore remains an important growth engine.

However, capital intensity increased in the historical dataset:

- Capex per net new restaurant increased from approximately $1.86M in 2024 to $2.20M in 2025
- Pre-opening cost per net new restaurant increased from approximately $0.21M to $0.27M

These measures are directional because total capital expenditures include investments beyond new restaurant construction.

## Management Focus

Expansion should be governed by:

- Site quality
- New-unit AUV
- Ramp performance
- Restaurant-level margin
- Capital per opening
- Payback
- Hiring and training capacity
- Field-management capacity
- Supply-chain readiness

Management should increase opening velocity only where these indicators remain within acceptable thresholds.

---

# 3. Invest Selectively in Scalable Infrastructure

## Rationale

Infrastructure ranked third as a standalone investment because its direct financial return is less observable.

A modeled 0.50 percentage-point improvement in operating efficiency illustrates approximately $5.8M of potential annual operating-income impact.

However, this materially understates infrastructure’s potential strategic value.

Infrastructure can improve:

- Opening execution
- Training quality
- Restaurant consistency
- Field-management capacity
- Supply-chain performance
- Performance visibility
- Labor productivity
- Future expansion capacity

Infrastructure should therefore be treated as an enabling investment rather than a separate growth strategy.

## Management Focus

Investment should concentrate on infrastructure with a clear connection to restaurant productivity or expansion capacity, including:

- Restaurant operating systems
- Training systems
- Field-management capacity
- Supply-chain scalability
- Performance analytics
- Development processes
- Workflow automation

Management should avoid building corporate infrastructure ahead of demonstrated operational need.

---

# Recommended Resource Allocation Logic

The analysis does not provide enough evidence to recommend precise dollar allocations.

Instead, management should use a staged allocation framework.

## First Allocation Priority

Fund high-confidence same-store productivity initiatives where:

- The operational problem is measurable
- The implementation cost is understood
- The expected economic benefit can be quantified
- The customer experience is protected
- The intervention can scale across multiple restaurants

## Second Allocation Priority

Fund restaurant expansion where:

- Site economics meet required thresholds
- New-unit ramp performance remains strong
- Capital requirements remain within target ranges
- Training and field-management capacity are available
- Expansion does not create material operating strain

## Third Allocation Priority

Fund infrastructure where it removes a demonstrated constraint on:

- Restaurant productivity
- Expansion velocity
- Operating consistency
- Management capacity
- Supply-chain performance

---

# Decision Rules

Management should use four tests before committing incremental resources.

## Economic Test

Does the investment create an attractive risk-adjusted return relative to competing uses of capital?

## Capacity Test

Does the organization have the people, systems, and operating capacity required to execute successfully?

## Customer Test

Does the initiative preserve or improve the restaurant experience?

## Scalability Test

Will the investment continue creating value as the restaurant base becomes materially larger?

---

# Evidence and Confidence

## High Confidence

- CAVA continues to grow its restaurant footprint rapidly
- Corporate operating leverage has improved
- The existing restaurant base creates meaningful scale for productivity initiatives
- Restaurant expansion requires substantial capital

## Medium Confidence

- Same-store productivity could offer attractive incremental capital efficiency if the required implementation investment remains materially below the modeled cost of accelerated expansion
- Infrastructure investment can improve expansion quality and operating scalability

## Lower Confidence

- Precise productivity implementation costs
- Exact new-unit capital requirements
- Exact payback periods
- The financial contribution of individual infrastructure investments

These areas require additional internal operating data before management should make precise capital-allocation decisions.

---

# Executive Conclusion

CAVA does not face a choice between growth and efficiency.

The stronger strategy is to improve the economics of growth.

Management should use the existing restaurant base as a productivity platform, continue opening restaurants where unit economics remain attractive, and invest selectively in infrastructure that prevents organizational capacity from becoming the constraint on future expansion.

The objective over the next 12–24 months should be:

> Grow the footprint, increase the productivity of the existing system, and build only the infrastructure required to sustain both.
