# Strategic Growth & Operating Plan — Project Brief

## Case Company

CAVA Group, Inc.

## Project Type

Flagship — Strategy, Resource Allocation & Operating Planning

## Core Management Question

> How should CAVA allocate capital and organizational capacity over the next 12–24 months to maximize growth while preserving restaurant-level economics?

## Management Decision

Determine how CAVA should balance investment across:

1. New restaurant expansion
2. Same-store productivity and customer economics
3. Infrastructure and organizational capacity required to support scale

The final recommendation should identify which initiatives deserve priority, which should receive selective investment, and which should be deferred or sequenced later.

## Strategic Context

CAVA remains in a high-growth phase with substantial restaurant expansion opportunities.

The central strategic challenge is not whether CAVA should continue growing, but how aggressively it should deploy capital and organizational resources toward expansion while protecting restaurant-level profitability, execution quality, customer experience, and long-term scalability.

## Strategic Alternatives

### Alternative 1 — Accelerate New Restaurant Expansion

Deploy greater resources toward restaurant openings and geographic penetration.

Potential value drivers:

- Revenue growth
- Market-share expansion
- Geographic density
- Brand awareness
- Future operating leverage

Primary constraints:

- Capital requirements
- Real estate availability
- Construction capacity
- Pre-opening costs
- Management bandwidth
- Hiring and training
- Execution quality

---

### Alternative 2 — Prioritize Same-Store Productivity

Allocate more resources toward improving performance of the existing restaurant base.

Potential value drivers:

- Traffic growth
- Average unit volume
- Throughput
- Labor productivity
- Digital and loyalty adoption
- Menu economics
- Restaurant-level margin

Primary constraints:

- Diminishing returns
- Customer price sensitivity
- Operational complexity
- Technology investment
- Restaurant-level execution

---

### Alternative 3 — Invest in Infrastructure for Scalable Growth

Increase investment in capabilities that enable the restaurant base to scale efficiently.

Potential areas:

- Supply chain
- Technology
- Restaurant systems
- Workforce training
- Management capacity
- Site-development processes
- Operating standards
- Analytics and performance management

Potential value drivers:

- Lower execution risk
- Improved restaurant productivity
- Faster future expansion
- Stronger operating consistency
- Reduced organizational bottlenecks

Primary constraint:

Near-term investment may reduce current profitability without immediately producing visible revenue growth.

## Decision Criteria

Each alternative will be evaluated on:

1. Revenue-growth potential
2. Restaurant-level economic impact
3. Capital requirements
4. Expected ROI
5. Payback period
6. Execution difficulty
7. Organizational capacity requirements
8. Strategic fit
9. Customer impact
10. Principal risks
11. Time to value
12. Evidence strength

## Resource Constraints

Resource allocation should consider more than financial capital.

Relevant constraints include:

- Capital
- Headcount
- Field-management capacity
- Technical capacity
- Supply-chain capacity
- Real estate and construction capacity
- Executive attention
- Organizational change capacity

Current State
→ Growth Objective

→ Resource Requirements
→ Economic Impact

→ Prioritization
→ 12–24 Month Operating Plan
→ Executive Recommendation

## Required Analysis

### Current-State Diagnostic

Evaluate:

- Revenue growth
- Restaurant count and opening pace
- Same-restaurant sales growth
- Traffic
- Average unit volume
- Restaurant-level margin
- Operating expenses
- Capital expenditures
- Pre-opening costs
- Geographic expansion
- Relevant operating KPIs

### Strategic Alternative Analysis

For each alternative:

- Define the investment
- Identify the economic mechanism
- Estimate resource requirements
- Model financial impact
- Identify dependencies
- Assess execution difficulty
- Evaluate principal risks
- Define success metrics

### Prioritization

Compare alternatives using both quantitative and qualitative evidence.

The project should avoid creating false mathematical precision. Financial impact, evidence strength, strategic fit, feasibility, and risk should all influence the recommendation.

## Source Discipline

Maintain four distinct categories:

- **Reported** — CAVA disclosures or other sourced facts
- **Calculated** — metrics derived from reported data
- **Assumption** — scenario inputs introduced for modeling
- **Recommendation** — proposed management actions

Assumptions must never be presented as company guidance or forecasts.

## Definition of Done

The project is complete when it can clearly answer:

> Where should CAVA allocate incremental resources over the next 12–24 months, why should those areas receive priority, what economic value could they create, what resources are required, and what risks could prevent the strategy from succeeding?

The final project should remain a concise management case study rather than becoming a full equity-research model or exhaustive corporate strategy engagement.
