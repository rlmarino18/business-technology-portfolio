# Strategic Alternatives Framework

## Objective

Define the three strategic paths CAVA could emphasize over the next 12–24 months and establish how each alternative creates value, what resources it requires, and what risks it introduces.

---

## Alternative 1 — Accelerated Restaurant Expansion

### Strategic Thesis

Continue prioritizing rapid restaurant growth to capture market share, expand geographic presence, and compound revenue through a larger unit base.

### Primary Value Drivers

- New restaurant openings
- Geographic expansion
- Brand awareness
- Market share growth
- Revenue growth
- Future density benefits

### Required Resources

- Capital expenditures
- Real estate pipeline
- Construction capacity
- Restaurant opening teams
- Hiring and training
- Field management
- Supply chain capacity
- Executive attention

### Primary Economic Mechanism

More productive restaurant units increase total restaurant sales and restaurant-level profit.

### Key Success Metrics

- Net new restaurant openings
- New-unit AUV
- New-unit ramp time
- Restaurant-level margin
- Capex per opening
- Pre-opening cost per opening
- Payback period
- Opening schedule adherence

### Principal Risks

- Rising build costs
- Lower-quality sites
- Slower unit ramp
- Management bandwidth constraints
- Hiring and training pressure
- Margin dilution
- Supply chain strain
- Execution inconsistency

---

## Alternative 2 — Same-Store Productivity

### Strategic Thesis

Allocate more incremental resources toward increasing economic output from the existing restaurant base rather than maximizing opening velocity.

### Primary Value Drivers

- Traffic growth
- Higher AUV
- Improved throughput
- Labor productivity
- Digital and loyalty adoption
- Menu innovation
- Customer frequency
- Restaurant-level margin improvement

### Required Resources

- Restaurant technology
- Operations improvement
- Training
- Labor optimization
- Digital product investment
- Loyalty and customer analytics
- Menu and pricing capabilities

### Primary Economic Mechanism

Generate more revenue and restaurant-level profit from existing locations without requiring equivalent new-unit capital investment.

### Key Success Metrics

- Same-restaurant sales growth
- Traffic growth
- AUV
- Restaurant-level margin
- Transactions per labor hour
- Digital mix
- Loyalty engagement
- Customer frequency

### Principal Risks

- Diminishing returns
- Customer price sensitivity
- Operational disruption
- Technology implementation risk
- Increased restaurant complexity
- Slower total footprint growth

---

## Alternative 3 — Infrastructure for Scalable Growth

### Strategic Thesis

Invest ahead of growth in the systems, people, processes, and infrastructure required to support a much larger restaurant base efficiently.

### Primary Value Drivers

- Improved execution consistency
- Faster future expansion
- Better restaurant productivity
- Reduced operating bottlenecks
- Lower organizational risk
- Stronger management visibility
- Better scalability

### Required Resources

- Technology investment
- Supply chain investment
- Analytics
- Management hiring
- Field leadership
- Training infrastructure
- Restaurant systems
- Development process improvement
- Corporate support capacity

### Primary Economic Mechanism

Increase the capacity and efficiency of the organization so future restaurant and revenue growth can occur with less operational friction and lower execution risk.

### Key Success Metrics

- G&A as a percentage of revenue
- New-unit opening performance
- Training readiness
- Time to open
- Restaurant operating consistency
- Supply chain service levels
- Management span of control
- Productivity metrics
- Margin stability

### Principal Risks

- Higher near-term expense
- Delayed financial return
- Overbuilding infrastructure
- Difficult benefit attribution
- Organizational complexity
- Investment ahead of actual demand

---

# Cross-Alternative Decision Criteria

Each strategic alternative will be evaluated on:

1. Revenue Growth Potential
2. Profit Impact
3. Capital Requirements
4. Expected ROI
5. Payback Period
6. Time to Value
7. Execution Difficulty
8. Organizational Capacity Requirements
9. Strategic Fit
10. Customer Impact
11. Principal Risks
12. Evidence Strength
13. Management Control

---

# Key Strategic Trade-Off

The decision is not whether CAVA should expand.

The decision is:

> How should CAVA balance the pace of restaurant expansion with investment in existing-store productivity and organizational infrastructure so that growth remains economically attractive and operationally sustainable?

---

# Modeling Approach

Each alternative will be tested using a low, base, and high scenario.

Scenario assumptions will be explicitly labeled and will not be presented as CAVA guidance or forecasts.

The analysis will compare:

- Incremental investment
- Incremental revenue
- Incremental restaurant-level profit
- Incremental operating income
- Payback
- Time to value
- Execution risk
- Confidence level
