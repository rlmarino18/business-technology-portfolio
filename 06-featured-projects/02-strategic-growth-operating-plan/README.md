# Strategic Growth & Operating Plan

**Type:** Flagship  
**Status:** Complete  
**Primary signal:** Strategy + resource allocation + execution planning

Uses CAVA public-company data to evaluate how management should balance restaurant expansion, same-store productivity, and scalable infrastructure over the next 12–24 months.

The project includes scenario modeling, capital-allocation trade-offs, prioritization analysis, and a phased operating plan designed to preserve growth while protecting unit economics and organizational capacity.

**Core management question:**

> How should CAVA allocate capital and organizational capacity over the next 12–24 months to maximize growth while preserving restaurant-level economics?

---

## Business Context

CAVA has continued expanding its restaurant footprint while maintaining strong restaurant-level economics.

However, the growth model is evolving.

Between 2023 and 2025:

- Revenue increased from approximately $717M to $1.17B
- Restaurant count increased from 309 to 439
- Same-restaurant sales growth declined from 17.9% to 4.0%
- Restaurant-level margin remained approximately 24–25%
- G&A declined from 14.2% to 11.8% of revenue
- Operating margin increased from approximately 0.7% to 4.7%

The company therefore faces a classic growth-stage allocation problem:

> Continue maximizing footprint expansion, extract more value from the existing restaurant base, or invest more heavily in the infrastructure required to support future scale?

---

The project follows this decision sequence:

**Current State → Growth Objective → Strategic Alternatives → Resource Requirements → Economic Impact → Risk & Feasibility → Prioritization → Operating Plan → Executive Recommendation**

# Strategic Alternatives

## 1. Accelerated Restaurant Expansion

### Base-Case Scenario

The model assumes 20 incremental restaurants above the baseline opening plan.

Estimated mature economics:

- $62.0M Incremental Annual Revenue
- $14.5M Incremental Restaurant-Level Profit
- $49.3M Modeled Initial Investment
- Approximately 3.4 Years Mature Payback

### Strategic Advantage

Expansion provides the largest modeled direct revenue and profit opportunity.

This sensitivity illustrates one potential financial outcome of improved operating leverage. It does not estimate the total ROI of infrastructure investment because implementation costs and broader benefits such as management capacity, training quality, supply-chain resilience, and operating consistency are not publicly observable.

## Primary Constraint

The strategy requires substantial capital and organizational capacity.

Historical indicators also suggest rising capital intensity:

- Capex per net new restaurant increased from approximately $1.86M in 2024 to $2.20M in 2025
- Pre-opening cost per net new restaurant increased from approximately $0.21M to $0.27M

These are directional measures because total capital expenditures include investments beyond restaurant construction.

---

## 2. Same-Store Productivity

### Base-Case Scenario

The model assumes a 3% productivity improvement across approximately 500 restaurants.

Estimated annual economics:

- $46.5M Incremental Revenue
- $10.9M Incremental Restaurant-Level Profit

### Strategic Advantage

The existing restaurant base allows relatively small productivity improvements to produce meaningful financial impact without requiring equivalent new-unit capital investment.

Potential value drivers include:

- Throughput
- Labor Productivity
- Customer Frequency
- Loyalty
- Digital Workflow Integration
- Menu Economics
- Restaurant-Level Execution

### Primary Constraint

The implementation cost required to generate the productivity improvement is not observable from public data.

The scenario should therefore be treated as a sensitivity analysis rather than a forecast.

---

## 3. Scalable Infrastructure

### Base-Case Scenario

The model assumes a 0.50 percentage-point improvement in corporate operating efficiency.

Estimated annual impact:

- Approximately $5.8M of illustrative annual operating-income impact under a 0.50 percentage-point efficiency scenario

### Strategic Advantage

Infrastructure can improve both restaurant productivity and expansion quality through:

- Field-Management Capacity
- Training
- Restaurant Systems
- Supply Chain
- Performance Analytics
- Development Processes
- Workflow Automation

### Primary Constraint

Infrastructure produces less observable standalone financial impact and can require investment before benefits become visible.

Its primary value is therefore as an enabling layer for the other two strategies.

---

# Prioritization Analysis

The alternatives were scored under three different management perspectives:

| Scenario | #1 Priority | #2 Priority | #3 Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Balanced | Same-Store Productivity | Accelerated Expansion | Scalable Infrastructure |
| Growth-First | Same-Store Productivity | Accelerated Expansion | Scalable Infrastructure |
| Efficiency-First | Same-Store Productivity | Accelerated Expansion | Scalable Infrastructure |

Same-store productivity ranked first under all three weighting scenarios.

However, under the Growth-First scenario, the difference between Same-Store Productivity and Accelerated Expansion was narrow.

This suggests that the optimal strategy is not a binary choice.

---

# Recommendation

CAVA should pursue a balanced growth strategy with the following priority:

## Priority 1 — Improve Same-Store Productivity

Use the existing restaurant network as a productivity platform.

Focus on increasing economic output per location while protecting customer experience and restaurant-level margins.

## Priority 2 — Maintain Disciplined Restaurant Expansion

Continue expanding the restaurant footprint where:

- Site Economics Remain Attractive
- New-Unit Ramp Performance Is Strong
- Capital Requirements Remain Within Target Ranges
- Hiring and Training Capacity Are Available
- Field-Management Capacity Can Support Growth

## Priority 3 — Invest Selectively in Scalable Infrastructure

Fund infrastructure where it directly removes demonstrated constraints on:

- Restaurant Productivity
- Expansion Capacity
- Operating Consistency
- Management Capacity
- Supply-Chain Performance

# 12–24 Month Operating Plan

## Phase 1 — 0–6 Months

Establish the operating baseline and identify the highest-return productivity opportunities.

Key actions:

- Diagnose Restaurant-Level Productivity Variation
- Identify Throughput and Labor Bottlenecks
- Review New-Unit Ramp Performance
- Establish Capital-Efficiency Benchmarks
- Assess Field-Management Capacity
- Identify Infrastructure Constraints

## Phase 2 — 6–12 Months

Scale validated productivity initiatives while maintaining disciplined restaurant growth.

Key actions:

- Deploy Proven Productivity Initiatives
- Continue Planned Restaurant Openings
- Invest Selectively in Enabling Technology and Training
- Monitor Unit Economics by Restaurant Cohort
- Track Capital Efficiency
- Adjust Opening Pace Where Capacity Becomes Constrained

## Phase 3 — 12–24 Months

Scale the operating model while protecting growth quality.

Key actions:

- Expand Successful Productivity Programs Systemwide
- Increase Development Where Economics Remain Attractive
- Strengthen Supply Chain and Field Leadership
- Standardize Scalable Operating Processes
- Reallocate Capital Based on Observed ROI
- Reassess Expansion Pace Against Organizational Capacity

---

# Executive Decision Rule

CAVA should accelerate investment only when three conditions are satisfied:

1. Unit Economics Remain Attractive
2. Organizational Capacity Can Support Additional Scale
3. Incremental Capital Produces Better Risk-Adjusted Returns Than Competing Uses

The objective is not simply to maximize growth.

The objective is to maximize the quality and economic value of growth.

---

# Key Findings

- CAVA continues to generate strong growth while showing improving corporate operating leverage.
- Same-restaurant sales growth has become less consistent as the restaurant base expands.
- Restaurant-level margins remain strong but should be monitored as growth continues.
- Expansion remains economically attractive but requires significant capital.
- Small productivity improvements across a large restaurant base can create meaningful financial value.
- Infrastructure should be treated primarily as an enabler of scalable growth rather than a standalone strategy.
- Resource allocation should remain dynamic and respond to actual ROI, operating capacity, and unit economics.

---

# Limitations

This analysis uses publicly available financial and operating information and therefore cannot observe many internal variables required for a full investment decision.

Key limitations include:

- Total Capex Is Used as a Proxy for New-Unit Investment
- New-Unit AUV and Ramp Curves Are Not Fully Observable
- Productivity Initiative Costs Are Unknown
- Infrastructure Investment Costs Are Unknown
- Restaurant-Level Performance Variation Is Not Available
- Internal Labor, Supply-Chain, and Field-Management Capacity Data Are Not Available

Scenario outputs should therefore be interpreted as decision-support sensitivities rather than forecasts.
