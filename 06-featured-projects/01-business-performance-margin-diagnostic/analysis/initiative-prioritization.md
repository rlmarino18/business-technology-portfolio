# Initiative Prioritization

## Objective

Select the highest-value management initiatives for financial modeling and executive prioritization based on the completed 2023–2025 performance diagnosis.

---

## Initiative 1 — Increase Subscription Product Adoption per Location

### Strategic Rationale

Subscription revenue is Toast's highest-margin reported revenue category and increased both its revenue mix and gross margin from 2023 to 2025.

Public disclosures indicate that subscription growth has been supported by both location growth and increased product adoption.

### Primary Value Mechanism

Increase recurring subscription revenue and gross profit per location through broader software adoption, packaging, and selective monetization.

### Initial Assessment

- Financial impact potential: High
- Evidence strength: Medium-High
- Execution difficulty: Medium
- Customer risk: Medium
- Management control: High

### Status

Advance to financial modeling.

---

## Initiative 2 — Improve Hardware & Services Unit Economics

### Strategic Rationale

Hardware & Services remained materially negative gross margin throughout 2023–2025 and represented a drag on consolidated gross profitability.

The objective is not necessarily to maximize standalone hardware margin. Management must preserve any strategic role hardware and professional services play in customer acquisition and activation.

### Primary Value Mechanism

Reduce gross loss per deployment through pricing, procurement, fulfillment, installation, configuration, and service-delivery improvements.

### Initial Assessment

- Financial impact potential: Medium-High
- Evidence strength: High for the financial problem; Medium for underlying causes
- Execution difficulty: Medium-High
- Customer risk: Medium-High
- Management control: Medium-High

### Status

Advance to financial modeling.

---

## Initiative 3 — Preserve Operating Leverage Through Functional Productivity

### Strategic Rationale

Operating-expense leverage accounted for approximately 64.6% of Toast's 2023–2025 operating-margin improvement.

G&A and R&D produced the largest expense-ratio improvements, although part of the historical improvement followed restructuring actions and should not be assumed to repeat indefinitely.

### Primary Value Mechanism

Maintain revenue and gross-profit growth above functional expense growth through prioritization, process productivity, automation, organizational discipline, and selective investment.

### Initial Assessment

- Financial impact potential: High
- Evidence strength: High
- Execution difficulty: Medium
- Organizational risk: Medium-High
- Management control: High

### Status

Advance to financial modeling.

---

## Portfolio Modeling Scope

The financial model will evaluate three management initiatives:

1. Increase subscription product adoption per location
2. Improve Hardware & Services unit economics
3. Preserve operating leverage through functional productivity

Each initiative will be assessed on:

- financial impact
- implementation cost
- payback period
- execution difficulty
- strategic fit
- customer impact
- dependencies
- principal risks
- evidence strength
- confidence level

Scenario assumptions will be explicitly labeled as assumptions rather than reported company guidance.

## Initiative 1 Financial Model — Subscription Product Adoption

### 2025 Baseline

- Subscription revenue: $936M
- Locations: approximately 164,000
- Calculated subscription revenue per location: approximately $5,707 annually
- Subscription gross margin: 71.79%

### Sensitivity Results

| Scenario | Revenue per Location Uplift | Incremental Subscription Revenue | Incremental Gross Profit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 2% | $18.72M | $13.44M |
| Base | 5% | $46.80M | $33.60M |
| High | 8% | $74.88M | $53.76M |

### Payback Sensitivity

At the 5% uplift scenario:

- $10M implementation cost: 3.6-month payback
- $20M implementation cost: 7.1-month payback
- $30M implementation cost: 10.7-month payback

Approximate subscription-revenue uplift required to achieve a 12-month payback:

- $10M implementation cost: 1.5%
- $20M implementation cost: 3.0%
- $30M implementation cost: 4.5%

### Management Interpretation

The modeled economics are attractive if Toast can generate approximately 3% or more incremental subscription revenue per location while keeping implementation cost near or below $20M.

The model should be treated as a sensitivity analysis rather than a forecast. Public disclosures do not provide sufficient information to estimate the actual attainable attach-rate improvement, implementation cost, or customer response.

### Current Assessment

- Financial attractiveness: High
- Evidence strength: Medium-High
- Key uncertainty: attainable adoption uplift
- Primary risk: customer resistance or insufficient incremental adoption
- Modeling confidence: Medium

## Initiative 2 Financial Model — Hardware & Services Unit Economics

### 2025 Baseline

- Hardware & Services revenue: $180M
- Hardware & Services cost: $400M
- Gross profit: -$220M
- Gross margin: -122.22%
- Gross-loss improvement pool: $220M

### Sensitivity Results

| Scenario | Gross-Loss Reduction | Gross-Profit Improvement | Resulting Gross Profit | Resulting Gross Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 10% | $22M | -$198M | -110.0% |
| Base | 20% | $44M | -$176M | -97.8% |
| High | 30% | $66M | -$154M | -85.6% |

### Payback Sensitivity

At the 20% gross-loss reduction scenario:

- $10M implementation cost: 2.7-month payback
- $20M implementation cost: 5.5-month payback
- $30M implementation cost: 8.2-month payback

Approximate gross-loss reduction required to achieve a 12-month payback:

- $10M implementation cost: 4.5%
- $20M implementation cost: 9.1%
- $30M implementation cost: 13.6%

### Management Interpretation

The modeled economics suggest that even modest improvement in Hardware & Services losses could create meaningful gross-profit value.

The objective should not necessarily be to make Hardware & Services profitable. Management should determine how much of the current loss represents remediable operational inefficiency versus intentional investment in customer acquisition, activation, or downstream recurring revenue.

Any optimization program should therefore evaluate the full customer economics rather than the segment gross margin in isolation.

### Current Assessment

- Financial attractiveness: High
- Evidence strength: High for the size of the financial problem
- Key uncertainty: proportion of losses that are economically intentional
- Primary risk: damaging customer acquisition or activation economics
- Modeling confidence: Medium

## Initiative 3 Financial Model — Operating Leverage Through Functional Productivity

### 2025 Baseline

- Revenue: $6.153B
- Total operating expenses: $1.301B
- Operating expenses as a percentage of revenue: 21.14%
- Operating income: $292M
- Operating margin: 4.75%

### Sensitivity Results

| Scenario | OpEx Efficiency Improvement | Operating Income Improvement | Resulting Operating Income | Resulting Operating Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 0.5 pp | $30.8M | $322.8M | 5.25% |
| Base | 1.0 pp | $61.5M | $353.5M | 5.75% |
| High | 1.5 pp | $92.3M | $384.3M | 6.25% |

### Management Interpretation

Even modest additional operating leverage could create meaningful operating-income improvement because Toast now operates at substantial revenue scale.

The base scenario indicates that a 1.0 percentage-point improvement in operating-expense efficiency would increase annual operating income by approximately $61.5M.

The opportunity should not be interpreted as a mandate for broad cost reduction. Management should focus on functional productivity, process improvement, automation, resource prioritization, and selective investment while protecting capabilities required for continued growth.

Historical operating leverage should not be extrapolated mechanically because part of the 2023–2025 improvement followed restructuring activity and future growth may require renewed investment.

### Current Assessment

- Financial attractiveness: High
- Evidence strength: High
- Key uncertainty: sustainable attainable efficiency improvement
- Primary risk: underinvestment in growth, product, or organizational capability
- Modeling confidence: Medium-High

## Prioritization Rationale

### Priority 1 — Preserve Operating Leverage

This initiative ranks first because it combines the largest modeled base-case financial impact with the strongest historical evidence. Operating-expense leverage accounted for approximately 64.6% of Toast's 2023–2025 operating-margin improvement, and a further 1.0 percentage-point efficiency improvement would increase annual operating income by approximately $61.5M at the 2025 revenue base.

The recommendation is not broad cost cutting. Management should focus on sustaining functional productivity through process improvement, automation, resource prioritization, and disciplined growth in operating expenses.

### Priority 2 — Increase Subscription Product Adoption per Location

This initiative ranks second because it is aligned with Toast's strongest reported segment economics and supports growth rather than relying primarily on cost reduction.

A 5% increase in subscription revenue per location would generate approximately $46.8M in incremental revenue and $33.6M in incremental gross profit at the 2025 baseline.

The primary uncertainty is whether this level of incremental product adoption or monetization is realistically attainable without increasing churn, discounting, or customer resistance.

### Priority 3 — Improve Hardware & Services Unit Economics

This initiative ranks third despite attractive modeled economics because the strategic role of Hardware & Services is not fully observable from public disclosures.

A 20% reduction in the 2025 Hardware & Services gross loss would generate approximately $44M in gross-profit improvement. However, management should first determine how much of the current loss represents remediable operational inefficiency versus intentional investment in customer acquisition and activation.

Optimization should therefore target clearly inefficient costs rather than attempt to maximize standalone segment profitability.

## Recommended Management Sequence

1. Preserve and extend operating leverage without compromising growth capacity.
2. Increase high-margin subscription adoption and recurring revenue per location.
3. Selectively improve Hardware & Services economics after validating downstream customer lifetime-value effects.

This sequence balances financial impact, evidence strength, growth preservation, execution risk, and management control.
