# Improvement Opportunity Framework

## Purpose

Translate the financial diagnosis into a disciplined set of management opportunities for further evaluation.

At this stage, these are candidate opportunities rather than recommendations. Each opportunity must be supported by evidence and evaluated for financial impact, implementation requirements, risk, and feasibility before prioritization.

---

## Opportunity 1 — Subscription Mix and Monetization

### Observed Performance

- Subscription revenue increased from $500M in 2023 to $936M in 2025.
- Subscription revenue mix increased from 12.94% to 15.21%.
- Subscription gross margin increased from 66.80% to 71.79%.
- Greater subscription mix contributed positively to overall gross-margin expansion.

### Management Lever

Increase high-margin recurring revenue per location through broader software-product adoption, packaging, selective monetization, and continued location growth. Public disclosures indicate that subscription growth has been driven by both growth in Toast locations and increased product adoption; pricing should therefore be evaluated as one lever rather than assumed to be the primary driver.

### Financial Mechanism

Higher subscription revenue or mix could increase gross profit because subscription revenue carries substantially higher gross margins than Toast's other reported revenue categories.

### Evidence Needed

- Location growth and net additions
- ARR growth
- Product adoption and attach-rate indicators
- Revenue or ARR per location
- Pricing and packaging changes
- Retention and expansion indicators
- Evidence separating location-driven growth from monetization-driven growth

### Key Risks

- Customer price sensitivity
- Churn or reduced adoption
- Competitive response
- Over-monetization of existing customers

## Opportunity 2 — Hardware & Services Economics

### Observed Performance

- Hardware & Services generated negative gross profit throughout 2023–2025.
- Segment gross margin was -102.84% in 2023, -84.58% in 2024, and -122.22% in 2025.
- Revenue mix declined from 4.55% to 2.93%.
- Reduced exposure to this negative-margin segment contributed materially to overall gross-margin improvement.

### Management Lever

Improve hardware and professional-services unit economics through pricing, procurement, fulfillment, installation efficiency, configuration standardization, and service-delivery productivity while preserving the role these offerings may play in acquiring and activating higher-value recurring customers.

### Financial Mechanism

Reducing losses per deployment could improve gross profit without requiring equivalent revenue growth.

### Evidence Needed

- Hardware pricing and discounting practices
- Hardware procurement and fulfillment costs
- Installation and onboarding labor requirements
- Professional-services delivery model
- Customer acquisition economics
- Relationship between hardware deployment and downstream subscription/fintech value
- Evidence that negative hardware economics are intentional versus operational inefficiency

### Key Risks

- Higher upfront pricing could reduce customer acquisition
- Hardware losses may be intentionally subsidizing higher-value recurring revenue
- Public disclosures do not establish whether negative hardware economics are intentionally subsidized, so any customer-acquisition interpretation should remain a hypothesis until supported.

## Opportunity 3 — Operating-Expense Productivity

### Observed Performance

- Operating expenses declined from 29.00% of revenue in 2023 to 21.14% in 2025.
- G&A improved by approximately 3.78 percentage points of revenue.
- R&D improved by approximately 3.18 percentage points.
- Operating-expense leverage accounted for approximately 64.6% of the total operating-margin improvement.

### Management Lever

Preserve operating leverage by keeping functional expense growth below revenue and gross-profit growth through resource prioritization, process productivity, automation, organizational discipline, and selective investment rather than assuming historical cost reductions can continue indefinitely.

### Financial Mechanism

Allow revenue and gross profit to grow faster than operating expenses, increasing operating margin.

### Evidence Needed

- Headcount and employee-cost trends by function
- 2024 restructuring actions and completion status
- 2025 expense growth relative to revenue growth
- Functional productivity indicators
- Stock-based compensation trends
- Management commentary on future hiring and investment
- Evidence distinguishing restructuring-driven savings from sustainable scale efficiencies

### Key Risks

- Excessive cost discipline could constrain innovation
- Underinvestment in R&D could weaken product competitiveness
- G&A efficiency may have natural limits as the company scales
- Part of the historical improvement followed a 2024 restructuring program, so extrapolating the full 2023–2025 expense-ratio decline would overstate sustainable future leverage.
- Future growth may require renewed investment in product development, sales capacity, international expansion, or corporate infrastructure.

## Opportunity 4 — Commercial Efficiency

### Observed Performance

- Sales & Marketing declined from approximately 10.38% of revenue in 2023 to 9.28% in 2025.
- Revenue continued to grow strongly while S&M consumed a smaller percentage of revenue.

### Management Lever

Increase commercial productivity by improving sales efficiency, expanding adoption within the installed customer base, optimizing channel mix, and prioritizing segments and markets that generate stronger recurring economics per unit of commercial investment.

### Financial Mechanism

Generate more revenue and gross profit per dollar of commercial spending.

### Evidence Needed

- Net location additions
- ARR growth
- ARR or recurring gross profit per location
- Sales headcount and productivity
- Customer acquisition cost, if available
- Retention and expansion behavior
- Channel and segment economics
- Evidence separating improved sales productivity from simple revenue-scale effects

### Key Risks

- Public disclosures do not provide enough information to calculate customer acquisition cost precisely.
- Lower Sales & Marketing as a percentage of revenue may reflect scale effects rather than materially better sales productivity.
- Over-optimizing commercial spending could slow location growth or expansion into new markets.
- Growth in newer markets may initially require higher acquisition and enablement investment.

---

## Evaluation Criteria

Each candidate opportunity will ultimately be assessed on:

1. Estimated financial impact
2. Implementation cost
3. Payback period
4. Execution difficulty
5. Strategic fit
6. Customer impact
7. Key dependencies
8. Principal risks
9. Evidence strength
10. Confidence level
